# 1. Purpose

This document defines the business rules governing payments made by customers toward loans in PocketPal.

It establishes how the system must handle:

- payment registration;
- full payments;
- partial payments;
- installment payments;
- overdue payments;
- late fees;
- interest;
- principal;
- payment allocation;
- overpayments;
- payment reversals;
- payment history;
- collection records;
- financial integration;
- loan balance updates.

The purpose of these rules is to ensure that every payment is financially traceable and that PocketPal can always explain how a customer's payment affected their debt.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Every payment received from a customer must be treated as a financial event.

A payment must never simply reduce a single generic `debt` value.

Instead, every payment must be allocated between its financial components.

Conceptually:

```text
Customer Payment
       │
       ├── Late Fees
       ├── Interest
       └── Principal

The exact allocation order is defined by the rules in this document.

3. Payment Components

A loan payment may affect three primary financial components:

PRINCIPAL
INTEREST
LATE_FEE

These components must remain separately identifiable.

Example:

Payment:
$150,000

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$20,000

Principal:
$120,000

The system must be able to explain the allocation:

$150,000
=
$10,000 late fee
+
$20,000 interest
+
$120,000 principal
4. Payment Amount

The amount entered for a payment must be greater than zero.

Required invariant:

payment_amount > 0

A payment of:

$0

must be rejected.

Negative payments must also be rejected.

Payment reversals are handled through a dedicated reversal operation rather than negative payment creation.

5. Payment Identity

Every payment must have a unique identifier.

Conceptually:

payment_id

This identifier must remain stable throughout the payment's lifecycle.

A payment must never be identified only by:

amount;
date;
customer;
loan.

Two legitimate payments can have the same amount and date.

6. Payment Structure

A payment should contain at least:

id
loan_id
customer_id

amount
payment_date

payment_method
reference
notes

status

created_at
updated_at

The payment allocation should be represented separately or in a structure that clearly identifies:

principal_amount
interest_amount
late_fee_amount
7. Payment Status

Initial payment statuses:

POSTED
REVERSED
POSTED

The payment is valid and contributes to the loan's financial state.

REVERSED

The payment was previously posted but has subsequently been reversed.

A reversed payment must not continue reducing the customer's outstanding balance.

8. Payment Date

Every payment must have a payment date.

The payment date represents when the payment was considered received.

Example:

payment_date = 2026-08-21

System timestamps such as:

created_at
updated_at

must not replace the payment date.

9. Payment Method

The payment method is descriptive metadata.

Initial supported methods:

CASH
BANK_TRANSFER
CARD
OTHER

The payment method does not determine payment allocation.

A cash payment and a bank transfer of the same amount follow the same financial rules.

10. Payment Reference

Payments may contain an external or user-defined reference.

Examples:

Transfer #12345
Nequi transaction
Receipt 00152

The reference should help identify the real-world payment.

It must not be used as the primary payment identifier.

11. Payment Allocation

When a payment is registered, the system must determine how much is applied to:

late fees
interest
principal

For v1.0, the default allocation priority is:

1. Late fees
2. Interest
3. Principal

Therefore:

Payment
   ↓
Late Fees
   ↓
Interest
   ↓
Principal

This order must be implemented consistently throughout the system.

12. Reason for Allocation Priority

Late fees represent overdue charges.

Interest represents the financial charge generated by the loan.

Principal represents recovery of the original capital.

Therefore, when a customer makes a payment, PocketPal must first settle outstanding late fees, then interest, and finally principal.

This avoids incorrectly treating unpaid charges as principal recovery.

13. Full Payment

A full payment occurs when the received amount is sufficient to completely satisfy the targeted outstanding obligation.

Example:

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$20,000

Principal:
$100,000

Total:
$130,000

Customer pays:

$130,000

Allocation:

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$20,000

Principal:
$100,000

Remaining obligation:

$0

The corresponding installment becomes:

PAID
14. Partial Payment

A partial payment occurs when the payment is insufficient to satisfy the targeted obligation.

Example:

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$20,000

Principal:
$100,000

Total:
$130,000

Customer pays:

$50,000

Allocation:

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$20,000

Principal:
$20,000

Remaining:

Principal:
$80,000

The installment remains:

PARTIAL

if its due amount is not completely satisfied.

15. Partial Payment Example — Payment Does Not Reach Principal

Suppose:

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$20,000

Principal:
$100,000

Customer pays:

$20,000

Allocation:

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$10,000

Principal:
$0

Remaining:

Interest:
$10,000

Principal:
$100,000

This is valid.

The system must not force every payment to contain a principal component.

16. Payment Smaller Than Late Fees

Suppose:

Late fee:
$20,000
Interest:
$10,000
Principal:
$100,000

Customer pays:

$5,000

The entire payment is allocated to the late fee:

Late fee paid:
$5,000

Interest paid:
$0

Principal paid:
$0

Remaining:

Late fee:
$15,000

Interest:
$10,000

Principal:
$100,000
17. Payment Allocation Formula

For a payment:

P

and outstanding components:

L = outstanding late fees
I = outstanding interest
C = outstanding principal

the allocation is:

late_fee_paid =
MIN(P, L)

Remaining payment:

R1 =
P - late_fee_paid

Interest allocation:

interest_paid =
MIN(R1, I)

Remaining payment:

R2 =
R1 - interest_paid

Principal allocation:

principal_paid =
MIN(R2, C)

Remaining amount:

remaining_payment =
R2 - principal_paid
18. Allocation Invariant

Every posted payment must satisfy:

payment_amount
=
late_fee_paid
+
interest_paid
+
principal_paid
+
unallocated_amount

For a normal payment without overpayment:

unallocated_amount = 0

Therefore:

payment_amount
=
late_fee_paid
+
interest_paid
+
principal_paid
19. Payment Cannot Reduce a Component Below Zero

The allocation engine must never produce:

principal_remaining < 0

or:

interest_remaining < 0

or:

late_fee_remaining < 0

Example:

Principal outstanding:
$50,000

Payment allocated to principal:
$50,000

Result:

Principal outstanding:
$0

Never:

-$10,000
20. Payment to a Specific Installment

For v1.0, a payment should be associated with a loan and may optionally target a specific installment.

When a specific installment is targeted, the allocation engine must first evaluate the outstanding components of that installment.

Example:

Loan #001

Installment #4
Due:
2026-08-20

Payment:

$100,000

The payment may be explicitly registered against installment #4.

21. Payment Without Explicit Installment

If the user registers a payment against a loan without specifying an installment, the system must determine the applicable installment according to the collection/payment strategy.

Default v1.0 strategy:

Oldest outstanding obligation first.

This means the system should prioritize the earliest unpaid installment.

22. Oldest Obligation First

Suppose a loan has:

Installment 1 — overdue
Installment 2 — overdue
Installment 3 — current
Installment 4 — future

A payment without a specific installment target should first apply to:

Installment 1

Once installment 1 is fully satisfied, any remaining payment may continue to:

Installment 2

and so forth.

23. Payment Across Multiple Installments

A single payment may satisfy multiple installments.

Example:

Installment 1:
$50,000

Installment 2:
$50,000

Installment 3:
$50,000

Customer pays:

$120,000

The system may allocate:

Installment 1:
$50,000

Installment 2:
$50,000

Installment 3:
$20,000

Installment statuses become:

Installment 1 → PAID
Installment 2 → PAID
Installment 3 → PARTIAL
24. Payment Allocation Across Installments

When a payment spans multiple installments, the system must:

1. Select the oldest outstanding installment.
2. Allocate payment according to component priority.
3. Determine remaining payment.
4. If payment remains, move to the next outstanding installment.
5. Repeat until payment is exhausted.

Conceptually:

Payment
   ↓
Oldest installment
   ↓
Late fee
   ↓
Interest
   ↓
Principal
   ↓
Remaining payment?
   │
   ├── No → Finish
   │
   └── Yes
        ↓
Next installment
25. Future Installments

A payment should not normally be allocated to future installments while earlier obligations remain outstanding.

Example:

Installment 1 — overdue
Installment 2 — overdue
Installment 3 — future

The payment should first settle installments 1 and 2.

Only after those obligations are satisfied may the remaining amount be considered for future obligations according to the overpayment/prepayment rules.

26. Prepayment

A customer may pay more than the amount currently due.

This is considered a prepayment situation.

Example:

Current outstanding:
$100,000

Customer pays:
$150,000

The extra:

$50,000

must not be silently discarded.

It must be handled according to the overpayment policy.

27. Overpayment Policy

For v1.0, the recommended behavior is:

Apply the payment to all currently outstanding obligations first.

Any remaining amount becomes:
PREPAYMENT / CREDIT

The credit must remain associated with the loan/customer.

Example:

Outstanding:
$100,000

Payment:
$150,000

Applied:
$100,000

Credit:
$50,000
28. Credit Balance

If a customer has an overpayment credit, the system must expose it explicitly.

Example:

Loan balance:
$0

Customer credit:
$50,000

The credit must not be displayed as:

Negative debt:
-$50,000

unless a future accounting design explicitly requires that representation.

29. Credit Application

An existing credit may later be applied to future loan obligations according to explicit user/system action.

Example:

Credit:
$50,000

Next installment:
$80,000

Apply credit:

Credit used:
$50,000

Remaining installment:
$30,000

The installment becomes:

PARTIAL

if no additional payment is made.

30. No Silent Loss of Money

PocketPal must never accept a payment where part of the amount disappears without a trace.

For every posted payment:

payment amount
=
allocated amount
+
credit/unallocated amount

The system must always be able to explain where the money went.

31. Payment and Interest

A payment applied to interest reduces:

outstanding_interest

It does not reduce:

outstanding_principal

unless part of the payment is explicitly allocated to principal.

Example:

Interest outstanding:
$20,000

Payment:
$20,000

Result:

Interest:
$0

Principal:
unchanged
32. Payment and Principal

A payment allocated to principal reduces:

outstanding_principal

Example:

Principal:
$500,000

Principal payment:
$100,000

New principal:

$400,000
33. Payment and Late Fees

A payment allocated to late fees reduces:

outstanding_late_fees

It does not reduce principal.

Example:

Late fees:
$10,000

Payment:
$10,000

Result:

Late fees:
$0

Principal:
unchanged
34. Payment on an Overdue Installment

When an overdue installment receives a payment, the payment allocation must include all applicable outstanding components:

Late fee
Interest
Principal

The payment must not automatically eliminate the overdue status unless the installment's entire outstanding amount has been settled.

35. Clearing an Overdue Installment

An overdue installment becomes:

PAID

when:

outstanding_amount = 0

After the payment:

its overdue balance becomes zero;
its status changes to PAID;
its contribution to overdue totals becomes zero.
36. Loan Status After Payment

After every posted payment, the system must reevaluate the loan status.

Possible outcomes:

OVERDUE
ACTIVE
PAID

Example:

Loan before payment:
OVERDUE

Customer pays all overdue obligations but still has future installments:

Loan after payment:
ACTIVE

If all obligations are satisfied:

Loan after payment:
PAID
37. Payment Recalculation

After a payment is posted, the system must update the affected financial state.

At minimum:

payment
↓
payment allocation
↓
installment balances
↓
loan balances
↓
loan status
↓
collection metrics
↓
financial metrics

All affected values must remain consistent.

38. Payment Atomicity

Registering a payment must be an atomic financial operation.

The system must not end in a state where:

payment exists

but:

installment was not updated

or:

loan balance was not updated

or:

payment allocation is missing

The entire operation must succeed or fail as one logical transaction.

39. Payment Idempotency

The system must protect against duplicate payment registration caused by:

double-clicks;
network retries;
frontend retries;
API retries;
accidental repeated requests.

A payment operation should support an idempotency mechanism where appropriate.

Example:

idempotency_key

If the same request is submitted twice, the system must not create two financial payments.

40. Duplicate Payment Protection

The system must not assume that two payments with the same:

amount
date
customer

are duplicates.

A customer may legitimately make two identical payments.

Duplicate detection should rely on a reliable payment identifier/reference/idempotency mechanism rather than simple field comparison.

41. Payment Reversal

A posted payment may be reversed.

A reversal must not delete the original payment.

Instead:

Original payment
        ↓
REVERSED
        ↓
Financial effects undone

The original payment remains visible in history.

42. Reversal Invariant

If a payment of:

$100,000

was allocated:

Late fee:
$10,000

Interest:
$20,000

Principal:
$70,000

and is reversed, the system must restore:

Late fee outstanding:
+$10,000

Interest outstanding:
+$20,000

Principal outstanding:
+$70,000

subject to the current state and any subsequent valid financial events.

43. Reversing a Payment Must Not Delete History

The system must preserve:

original payment
payment date
amount
allocation
reversal date
reversal reason
user who performed reversal

This creates an auditable history.

44. Reversal After Subsequent Payments

A particularly important case occurs when:

Payment A

is followed by:

Payment B

and then:

Payment A

is reversed.

The system must not blindly subtract Payment A from the current loan balance.

The financial history must be reconstructed or adjusted in a deterministic manner.

The implementation must account for subsequent financial events.

This operation should be handled by a dedicated domain service.

45. Reversal Restrictions

A payment reversal should require:

authenticated user;
valid payment;
payment status POSTED;
explicit confirmation;
reversal reason.

A payment already marked:

REVERSED

cannot be reversed again.

46. Payment Notes

Users may add notes to payments.

Examples:

Paid in cash
Transfer confirmed
Customer paid early
Partial payment

Notes are descriptive and must not affect financial calculations.

47. Payment Receipt

The application may generate a payment receipt containing:

Customer
Loan
Payment date
Payment amount
Payment method

Late fee paid
Interest paid
Principal paid

Remaining balance
Reference

The receipt must reflect the authoritative backend allocation.

48. Payment History

Each loan must provide a chronological payment history.

Example:

2026-08-01
$100,000
Principal: $70,000
Interest: $20,000
Late fee: $10,000

2026-08-15
$80,000
Principal: $80,000
Interest: $0
Late fee: $0

The history must allow the user to understand how the debt evolved.

49. Customer Payment History

The customer profile should provide aggregated payment history across loans.

At minimum:

Total paid
Principal paid
Interest paid
Late fees paid
Last payment
Number of payments

These values must be derived from valid posted payments.

Reversed payments must not count toward active financial totals.

50. Daily Collection Metrics

PocketPal should support a daily collection view.

For a selected date:

Expected collections
Collected
Pending
Overdue

The system should distinguish:

scheduled amount
actual amount received
remaining amount
51. Today's Collection

For today's collection view, the application should identify:

Installments due today
+
Outstanding overdue installments

depending on the collection view selected by the user.

The UI should clearly distinguish:

DUE TODAY
OVERDUE

These are not the same state.

52. Collection Status

A collection-oriented view may expose:

TODAY
UPCOMING
OVERDUE
PAID

These are presentation/collection classifications and must not replace the authoritative installment statuses.

53. Financial Integration

A customer payment can have multiple financial effects.

Example:

Customer pays:
$150,000

Allocation:
$100,000 principal
$40,000 interest
$10,000 late fee

From the personal finance perspective:

Principal recovery:
$100,000
Interest income:
$40,000
Late fee income:
$10,000

The system must not record:

$150,000 personal income

as if the entire payment were profit.

54. Principal Recovery Is Not Income

This is one of the most important PocketPal rules.

If:

Principal recovered = $100,000

that does not mean:

Personal income = $100,000

It represents recovery of money previously lent.

Only the income-generating components should count as personal financial income.

55. Interest as Income

When interest is collected:

interest_paid > 0

the corresponding amount may be recorded as financial income.

Example:

Interest paid:
$40,000

Financial income:

+$40,000

The loan remains the authoritative source of the amount.

56. Late Fee as Income

When a late fee is collected:

late_fee_paid > 0

the corresponding amount may be recorded as financial income.

Example:

Late fee paid:
$10,000

Financial income:

+$10,000

The application should maintain a distinction between:

Interest income
Late fee income

for reporting.

57. Avoiding Double Counting in Personal Finance

Suppose:

Payment:
$150,000

Principal:
$100,000

Interest:
$40,000

Late fee:
$10,000

Personal finance income must be:

$50,000

not:

$150,000

The:

$100,000

principal component is recovery of capital.

Therefore:

Financial income
=
interest collected
+
late fees collected

for this loan-payment scenario.

62. Payment Against a Paid Loan

A normal payment should not be accepted against a loan whose obligations are already fully settled.

If a customer attempts to pay a paid loan, the system should require explicit handling of the amount as:

CREDIT

or reject the operation.

It must never silently increase a paid loan's principal.

63. Payment Against a Cancelled Loan

Payments against cancelled loans should normally be rejected.

If a real-world payment arrives after cancellation, the user must explicitly resolve the situation through a controlled process.

The system must not reactivate the loan automatically.

64. Payment Against an Inactive Customer

A customer's active/inactive status must not automatically erase financial obligations.

If the customer becomes inactive:

Existing loans remain.
Existing payments remain.
Historical records remain.

A payment may still be registered if the loan itself permits it.

65. Transaction Consistency

A payment operation must preserve consistency across:

Payment
Payment allocation
Installment
Loan
Customer
Personal finance records

A failure in one required component must not leave the system partially updated.

66. Database Transaction

The backend should use a database transaction for payment posting.

Conceptually:

BEGIN TRANSACTION

Create payment
Create allocation
Update installment
Update loan
Create financial effects
Update relevant aggregates

COMMIT

If any critical operation fails:

ROLLBACK
67. Concurrency

The system must protect against two payments being registered simultaneously against the same outstanding balance.

Example:

Outstanding principal:
$100,000

Two requests simultaneously attempt:

Payment A:
$100,000

Payment B:
$100,000

The system must not allow both requests to independently conclude that $100,000 was available.

Concurrency control must ensure that the resulting financial state remains valid.

68. Concurrent Payment Invariant

After concurrent payment attempts:

outstanding_principal >= 0

must remain true.

The system must not produce:

outstanding_principal = -$100,000

due to race conditions.

69. Audit Events

Important payment operations should create audit information.

At minimum:

PAYMENT_CREATED
PAYMENT_REVERSED
PAYMENT_CREDIT_APPLIED

Potential metadata:

user_id
payment_id
loan_id
timestamp
amount
reason
70. Payment Calculation Authority

The backend is the authoritative source for:

payment allocation
remaining balance
loan status
installment status
interest paid
principal paid
late fee paid

The frontend must not determine these values as authoritative.

71. API Behavior

A payment creation request should conceptually contain:

loan_id
amount
payment_date
payment_method
reference
notes

Optionally:

installment_id

if the user explicitly targets an installment.

The backend then determines the authoritative allocation.

A successful payment response should provide enough information for the UI to immediately display the result.

Example structure:

The exact API schema belongs to:

docs/technical/API.md
73. Error Conditions

Payment creation must reject or safely handle:

amount <= 0
loan does not exist
loan belongs to another user
loan is cancelled
invalid payment date
invalid payment method
duplicate idempotency key
invalid installment
concurrent state conflict
74. No Manual Balance Editing

Users must not directly edit:

outstanding principal
outstanding interest
outstanding late fees

These values must be derived from:

loan configuration
installments
payments
payment allocations
reversals

If a correction is necessary, it must be represented as a valid financial event.

75. Financial Correction

If an error occurs, the preferred mechanism is:

Correction Event

rather than directly modifying historical balances.

Example:

Incorrect payment:
$100,000

Corrective reversal:
$100,000

Correct payment:
$80,000

This is preferable to silently changing the original payment.

76. Payment History Immutability

Once a payment is posted, its historical allocation should not be silently modified.

If a correction is required:

Original event
+
Correction event

must preserve the history.

77. Reporting Rules

Payment reports should distinguish:

Total collected
Principal collected
Interest collected
Late fees collected
Credit generated
Reversed payments

A reversed payment must not remain included in active collection totals.

78. Daily Payment Report

For a selected day, PocketPal should be able to show:

Number of payments
Total collected
Principal recovered
Interest collected
Late fees collected
Credits generated
Reversed payments

Example:

Payments:
12

Collected:
$1,500,000

Principal:
$1,200,000

Interest:
$250,000

Late fees:
$50,000

The components must reconcile:

$1,500,000
=
$1,200,000
+
$250,000
+
$50,000
79. Reconciliation Invariant

For a set of valid posted payments:

Total collected
=
Total principal allocated
+
Total interest allocated
+
Total late fee allocated
+
Total credit allocated

Reversed payments must be excluded from active totals.

This invariant is essential for reporting accuracy.

80. Customer-Level Reconciliation

For each customer:

Total payments
=
Principal paid
+
Interest paid
+
Late fees paid
+
Credits

subject to reversed transactions being excluded from active totals.

81. Loan-Level Reconciliation

For each loan, the system must be able to reconcile:

Original principal
-
Principal recovered
=
Outstanding principal

And:

Generated interest
-
Interest paid
=
Outstanding interest

And:

Generated late fees
-
Late fees paid
=
Outstanding late fees

This gives the system an auditable financial model.

82. Payment Rule Priority

When payment rules conflict, priority is:

Financial integrity.
Explicit payment allocation rules.
Loan configuration.
Historical consistency.
Auditability.
User experience.

The application must never alter payment allocation simply because another allocation appears more convenient visually.

83. Versioning

This document represents:

PocketPal Payment Rules v1.0

Changes to:

payment allocation order;
overpayment behavior;
reversal behavior;
principal calculation;
interest treatment;
late-fee treatment;

must be considered business-critical changes.

Existing financial records must not silently change because a future version modifies these rules.

84. Final Principle

PocketPal must always be able to answer:

How much did the customer pay?

How much went to late fees?

How much went to interest?

How much went to principal?

Was the payment partial or complete?

Which installment did it satisfy?

How much does the customer still owe?

Was there an overpayment?

Was the payment later reversed?

How did this payment affect the user's personal finances?
